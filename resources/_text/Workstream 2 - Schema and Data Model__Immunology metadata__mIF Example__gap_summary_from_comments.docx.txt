# original kept: yes

Here\'s the consolidated summary of gaps between the mIF example data
and the aind-data-schema:

**Subject schema**

The schema has no type for histological specimens. All three subject
detail types (Mouse, Human, NonHumanPrimate) describe live subjects;
TissueSection needs to be added with fields for tissue/slide IDs,
fixation method, embedding medium, section thickness, and antigen
retrieval parameters. For the human subject type specifically: species
is redundant with the subject type and can be dropped; source is
designed around animal acquisition and doesn\'t translate to a human
collaborator/collection site; year\_of\_birth needs HIPAA-compliant
handling for subjects over 89 (aggregation, and a mechanism to update
this over time). Several fields present in the data have no home in the
schema at all: diagnosis (multi-value, updatable), ethnicity, race,
consent, and cohort --- all of which are more naturally attached to the
subject than to the acquisition.

**Data description schema**

subject\_id as the primary key breaks down when an acquisition covers
multiple specimens, or when the meaningful identifier is a sample rather
than a subject (e.g., a tissue section or a tube). The auto-generated
name field (subject\_id + timestamp) risks collision if two datasets are
created on the same day without a precise time. project\_name needs
clarification on whether it should be a human-readable name, an
experiment ID like EXP-01367, or both. modalities doesn\'t include mIF
or any cyclic/multiplexed imaging modality --- this is a registry gap,
not just a naming question. tags, source\_data, and restrictions all
lack guidance: what controlled vocabulary applies to tags, how
source\_data gets populated (manual vs. automated), and whether
restrictions should hold a legal document ID or overlap with consent
fields already in the subject schema.

**Acquisition schema**

The biggest structural gap: DataStream models simultaneous data
collection, but Cell DIVE (and any cyclic assay) is inherently
sequential --- 10 staining rounds, each with its own operator, date,
antibody plan, reagent lots, deactivation step, and QC flags. There\'s
no model for this cyclic structure. The antibody panel itself ---
channel assignments, antibody name, clone, fluorophore, concentration,
lot number, and barcode verification --- has no place anywhere in the
current schema. This gap applies beyond mIF: Xenium gene panels and Flex
scRNA have the same problem. subject\_details within acquisition is
mouse-centric (weight, platform, reward) and mislabeled --- for human
tissue experiments the relevant \"subject at acquisition\" information
is clinical or survey data (e.g., smoking history at time of
collection), which needs to be searchable. ethics\_review\_id is noted
as mouse-schema-specific and not applicable for human subjects.
calibrations and maintenance are questioned as better fits under QC than
acquisition. stimulus\_epochs and manipulations are entirely
inapplicable to fixed tissue experiments.

**Proposed additions (from the comments)**

Four new models were flagged as needed: TissueSection (subject type),
StainingRound (cyclic acquisition unit with antibody plan, deactivation,
and reagent log), AntibodyChannel (per-channel reagent metadata with
barcode verification), and DeactivationStep (reagent lots and protocol
parameters between rounds). mIF also needs to be added to the modality
registry, with a question about granularity --- whether \"Cell DIVE\"
should be its own modality or a platform-level detail within mIF.
